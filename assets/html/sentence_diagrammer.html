How to read it, and what this prototype can’t do
  • The main line holds the subject, verb, and object. A vertical line through the baseline separates subject and verb.
  • A short vertical line resting on the baseline marks a direct object. A backslash marks a complement after a linking verb (is, seems, became).
  • Slanted lines carry modifiers: articles, adjectives, and adverbs. A preposition sits on a slanted line that leads to its object.
  • Limits: one clause only. No questions, and/but/or, infinitives, or indirect objects. A prepositional phrase after the verb attaches to the verb, which is not always right. The vocabulary is a small built-in list plus spelling hints, so fix tags by clicking.
